### Start with the taxable event, not the bank exchange

For each item, identify when the income was earned, became payable, was actually paid, and was received or made available. Then apply the date rule for that income category. Exchanging USD, EUR, or another currency into won weeks later is a cash-management event and may not be the tax conversion date.

### Build a transaction-level schedule

Use columns for payer, income category, foreign amount, currency, agreed date, actual payment date, tax-recognition date, rate source, rate, KRW amount, foreign tax, and supporting file. A transparent schedule is easier to defend and correct than a single annual total.

### Treat salary timing carefully

NTS foreign-employee guidance distinguishes foreign-currency salary paid and converted before the agreed date from salary paid on or after that date. If payroll was delayed, advanced, netted, or paid through a foreign parent, retain the contract and payroll calendar and ask the employer which date and rate it used.

### Use one authoritative rate source consistently

Save the source name, URL or downloaded table, date, and whether the rate is a standard or arbitrated rate. Do not mix a card-company rate, retail cash rate, bank transfer rate, and spreadsheet average without an explanation.

### Reconcile income, basis, and credits

Foreign income, asset cost, sale proceeds, and foreign tax can use different relevant dates. Review the entire transaction chain so a currency gain, investment basis, or foreign tax credit is not calculated from incompatible conversions.
