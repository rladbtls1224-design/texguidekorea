### Use this page after the document has been selected

This checklist is for a packet that is nearly ready, or a certificate request that has failed. If you still need to distinguish income proof, clearance, and payment history, start with [choosing tax documents for visa extension](/guides/tax-documents-visa-extension-korea/).

Make a small issue log: requested document, requested year, actual result, who can resolve it, and your next follow-up date. This keeps a missing tax record from getting confused with a question about immigration acceptance.

### Check the year before investigating missing income

An **issue date** tells you when the certificate was produced. An **income year** tells you which year's income it covers. They need not be the same.

The [NTS certificate application guidance](https://www.nts.go.kr/taxpayer_advocate/na/ntt/selectNttInfo.do?nttSn=1302741) gives these release periods for regular filings:

| Certificate or applicant group | NTS stated release period for the relevant tax year |
| --- | --- |
| Income certificate for a comprehensive-income tax filer | From July of the following year |
| Income certificate for other applicants | From May of the following year |
| Fact certificate of no filing, 신고사실없음 | From July of the following year |

For example, those regular-filing periods point to May or July **2026** for **2025** income. They do not mean that 2026 income becomes a completed annual certificate during 2026. Check the actual selectable year and record.

The same NTS guidance says late-filed returns require the district tax office's review and determination before the relevant certificate can be issued; it describes a review period within three months of receipt. Do not promise the receiving office that filing today will make the certificate available immediately.

### Diagnose the result instead of ordering a substitute

| What you see | First checks | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requested year is unavailable | Income year, filing type, normal release period, late/corrected filing status | Ask the tax office whether the record is issuable; ask immigration what evidence is accepted meanwhile |
| One employer or client is missing | That payer's statement, taxpayer identification, year, and income category | Ask the payer about the reported record; keep its response and any corrected statement |
| Amount is lower than bank deposits | Gross receipts versus income amount; reimbursements, expenses, periods, and withholding | Reconcile each item rather than copying a bank total into a tax return |
| Name or identity differs | Passport spelling, registration details, old/new identifiers, payer's recorded information | Ask the responsible authority or payer which underlying record needs correction |
| Clearance cannot be issued | Exact certificate, issuing authority, status message, and any identified arrears | Resolve the stated tax-status issue with that authority; payment history is a different document |
| Office rejects the printout | Document name, language, year, expiry, required disclosure, and delivery format | Obtain the rejection reason and reissue only the item that needs correction |

These are troubleshooting prompts rather than findings about your tax position. A certificate problem can have more than one cause.

### Treat no-filing evidence carefully

"No certificate," "no filing record," and "no income" describe different things. If you actually received income, first investigate whether the correct payer record or tax return exists. Do not choose a no-filing fact certificate simply to fill an empty slot in a visa packet.

The NTS [certificate service information](https://www.nts.go.kr/english/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=238885&mi=40036) lists fact certificates among documents issued after departmental review. Confirm the exact fact-certificate type and covered period with the issuer. Separately ask the receiving office whether that evidence meets its request. A fact certificate does not decide whether a return was legally required or whether a visa income threshold was met.

### Run a final packet check

Before the appointment or upload, compare each page with the original request:

1. The correct person and Korean document title appear.
2. The income year or payment period matches the request.
3. The issue date and any printed validity date meet the receiving office's instructions.
4. Language, identity disclosure, and submission format match what was requested.
5. All pages, verification markings, and required attachments are legible and complete.
6. Any discrepancy has a separate explanation and supporting evidence; the official certificate itself has not been altered.

Use filenames such as `income-certificate-2025-issued-2026-10-26` to distinguish the two dates. Keep originals and working copies securely; share sensitive identifiers only through the institution's accepted route.

### Ask the office that can resolve the issue

For an income record or certificate issuance issue, take the error message, relevant year, payer statement, and filing receipt to the tax office. [Government24's income-certificate instructions](https://www.gov.kr/mw/AA020InfoCappView.do?CappBizCD=12100000021) describe identification and representative requirements; an online application cannot be made by a representative.

For whether an alternative is accepted, or whether a year, format, or deadline fits the visa application, use the handling immigration office or [Hi Korea's 1345 service](https://www.hikorea.go.kr/Main.pt?locale=en). Keep a note of the date and specific question. A tax-office explanation of issuance and an immigration decision on acceptance answer different parts of the problem.
