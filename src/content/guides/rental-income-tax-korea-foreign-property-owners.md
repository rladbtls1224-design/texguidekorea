### Separate each property and payment type

Use one ledger per property. Record ordinary monthly rent, maintenance reimbursements, parking or management income, deposits, vacancies, refunds, and amounts retained by an agent. Reconcile gross tenant payments to the net amount deposited in your bank account.

### Confirm residence and property facts

Your tax residence affects the wider income calculation, but Korean property remains a Korean-source item that a non-resident cannot simply ignore. Also record whether the property is housing or commercial, jointly owned, registered as a rental business, and how many homes are counted under the applicable rule.

### Review deposit and housing rules

Housing rental provisions can distinguish monthly rent from deposits and may use ownership-count, value, or deposit thresholds. Because these figures and special rules can change, use the current return-year guide and do not copy a prior owner's tax treatment.

### Build an evidence-based expense file

Keep invoices, proof of payment, and an explanation for repairs, insurance, management fees, property tax, professional fees, and financing costs. Separate improvements from repairs and principal from interest. Allocate shared costs consistently if only part of a property is rented.

### File and pay from the correct location

A resident may combine qualifying rental income with other aggregate income, while a non-resident may need a Korean filing contact or representative. Confirm both national income tax and the connected local income tax, and keep a Korean bank or payment plan in place if you live abroad.
