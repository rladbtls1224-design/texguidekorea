### Choose the return before entering figures

Start with two questions: which income year are you dealing with, and has a return for that year already been submitted? Keep the original receipt if there is one. A missing deduction, newly discovered income and a first late return are different problems; ask which correction or late-filing route fits before opening a new original return.

The [NTS overview](https://www.nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=7664&mi=2224) explains filing exceptions and deadlines. Salary-only workers whose year-end adjustment is complete may fall within an exception, but uncombined employers or other income require further checking. For the ordinary **2025 income-year** return, it lists **June 1, 2026**, with **June 30** for the required tax-compliance-confirmation group. As of this guide's October 27 review, those windows have passed. Do not interpret a payment extension as a new filing deadline without checking its terms.

### Use the verified starting path

The [official NTS filing instructions](https://www.nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=238910&mi=40296) give this starting path:

**Hometax → login → 세금신고 → 종합소득세 신고**

The last two labels mean tax filing and comprehensive income-tax filing. Select the correct return and filing type from the options presented. These directions follow published official guidance; your account and filing period may produce different subsequent screens.

Use the [NTS manual library](https://www.nts.go.kr/nts/na/ntt/selectNttList.do?bbsId=30000&mi=2243) for the specific method. It contains separate manuals, including late-filing materials. An English explanation of a term is not enough to decide whether an accounting or expense method applies to you.

| Your records show | What to settle before choosing the return |
| --- | --- |
| Salary from one employer | Whether year-end settlement is complete and whether any exception to separate filing applies |
| Salary from several employers | Whether earlier pay and withholding were combined by the final employer |
| Freelance or business receipts | The income classification, accounting method and supported expenses |
| Salary plus side work | How the different income categories are included without duplication |
| Overseas payments or work | Residence, source, treaty and foreign-tax questions before entering amounts |

### Make a reconciliation outside the form

Create a worksheet with payer, income type, income period, gross amount, withholding and source-document reference. Compare that worksheet with the imported records. Use the original statements to explain each difference; do not force a bank deposit to equal a gross-income field when a payer has withheld tax or deducted charges.

For deductions, retain the document and the reason you meet the applicable conditions. A prefilled figure or an available checkbox does not establish eligibility. If an amount remains unexplained, pause that part of the return and identify the missing evidence.

### Fictional example: an employer and a client

Sam has an employer withholding receipt and a separate client payment statement. The salary figures match Hometax, but the client does not appear. Sam records the discrepancy and asks the client for the reporting details. Sam does not assume that a salary-only filing is complete merely because that screen is already filled in.

This example illustrates preparation, not the tax classification or liability of a particular payment. The contract and applicable rules still determine how the client income is treated.

### Treat submission, local filing and payment as separate checkpoints

Before national submission, compare the final summary with your worksheet: identity, income year, income totals, withholding, supported deductions and payment or refund result. Save the submitted return and receipt. A stored draft or calculation screenshot is insufficient evidence that submission finished.

The [2026 local-tax guidance](https://www.seoul.go.kr/news/news_report.do?nttNo=458228) describes moving from the national return to Wetax through the local-tax filing link. Review the transferred details and keep the local service's own receipt. If the link fails, preserve the national receipt and resolve the local return with the relevant authority; do not submit the national return repeatedly to fix a transfer problem.

Finally, record any national and local amounts due, their deadlines and payment references separately. Use the [payment procedure](/procedures/how-to-pay-korean-tax-online/) for an amount due or the [refund-status procedure](/procedures/how-to-check-tax-refund-status/) for a refund result.
