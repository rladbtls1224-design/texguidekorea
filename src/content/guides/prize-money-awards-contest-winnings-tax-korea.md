### Sort the payment into the right lane

Use the payment's real cause:

| Situation | Likely starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Normal performance bonus or work-related reward | Wage and salary income |
| Prize for special merit in a genuine competition | Other income may apply |
| Private public contest award | Other income, subject to category and expense rules |
| Qualifying government or specifically listed award | Check the exact non-taxable provision |
| Lottery or similar winnings | Separate statutory withholding treatment |

The organizer's label is useful evidence but does not override the facts.

### Ask for a gross-to-net statement

The winner should know the gross prize, any statutory expense amount used, national tax, local tax, and net payment. Request a withholding receipt rather than relying on the bank deposit. Non-cash prizes also need a value and tax explanation.

### Check whether withholding ends the matter

Some other income can be subject to final separate taxation, some may allow an election, and some must be combined in a comprehensive return. Lottery-type income can follow a different rule. The answer depends on the category and annual facts, so do not extend one contest's treatment to every award.

### Employee awards need extra care

If the recipient works for the payer, ask why the award was made. A routine sales incentive, service award, or compensation-like payment may be wages. A genuinely separate competition or special-merit prize may be analyzed differently. Keep the employer's plan and selection criteria.

### Nonresidents and cross-border winners

A foreign winner may still have Korean-source income and Korean withholding. Check the relevant domestic source rule and treaty rather than assuming foreign residence removes tax. Keep residence certificates and confirm whether the Korean withholding is final or refundable under an applicable procedure.
