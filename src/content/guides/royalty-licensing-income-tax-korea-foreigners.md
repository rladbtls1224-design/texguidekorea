### Identify what the payer receives

Read the grant clause, not only the invoice description. Does the payer receive permission to reproduce, distribute, modify, sublicense, broadcast, use a trademark, access source code, or apply confidential know-how? Or is the payer buying a finished product or receiving personal services? The contract can contain more than one component.

### Determine the source and recipient status

Analyze where the right is used, the domestic source rule, the recipient's Korean residence status, and whether the income is connected with a Korean business place. A foreign bank account or foreign contract signature does not by itself resolve the source question.

### Apply treaty procedure before payment

When a treaty offers a reduced royalty rate or different classification, the Korean payer usually needs valid residence and beneficial-owner documentation under the applicable procedure. Contracting parties should address documents, gross-up clauses, local income tax, and refund responsibility before the due date.

### Split bundled contracts reasonably

Software, media, franchise, and technical agreements often combine licenses, maintenance, customization, training, and equipment. Use the legal rights, pricing, deliverables, and economic substance to support any allocation. A single label or arbitrary percentage is weak evidence.

### Keep both sides of the tax record

The recipient should obtain the withholding receipt and reconcile gross income to net cash. Korean residents receiving royalties from abroad should review worldwide-income rules, currency conversion, deductible expenses, and foreign tax credits. Preserve any later refund or amended withholding information.
