### Build a three-record trail

Keep three records together: the submitted return or claim, the official refund notice or payment record, and the bank transaction. They answer different questions.

| Record | What it helps establish | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calculation or saved draft | What a calculation currently estimates | That a return was submitted |
| Submitted return and receipt | What was filed and when | That the money reached your bank |
| Official refund/payment record | What the authority has recorded about the refund | That every amount will arrive as one deposit |
| Bank statement | What actually entered the account | Which tax and income year it relates to without matching evidence |

If an employer is handling the refund through payroll, first ask for the year-end adjustment record and the payslip or transfer that should contain it. Do not assume an employer's payroll adjustment will appear as a personal tax-office transfer in the account you are checking.

### Use the right tax type and search dates

For a national tax-office refund, open [Hometax](https://www.hometax.go.kr/) directly and search for **환급금 상세조회** (detailed refund enquiry). Compare the information available in your result with your own records: tax office, tax type, refund amount, payment date, bank and attributable period. If the service offers a date-range filter, choose dates covering the expected processing or payment. Identify the record from several matching details rather than the amount alone. If the service is unavailable, keep the return reference and contact the responsible tax office instead of using an unfamiliar sign-in address.

The year income was earned can differ from the year a refund is paid. If a 2025 return was filed in 2026, a payment-date search may need 2026 dates. Widen the relevant search period before concluding that no record exists.

A personal local income-tax refund is a separate item. Use the relevant local authority or Wetax record for that item; an empty national result does not settle whether a local refund exists.

### Read the notice before changing the account

The [statutory national refund notice](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110202&flSeq=150100563&gubun=) explains that unpaid tax can be offset before the balance is returned. It also provides contacts for refund decisions/account reporting and for receipt/payment-date questions. Follow the contact relevant to the discrepancy.

The form gives this national refund-account route: **납부·고지·환급 → 국세환급 → 환급계좌개설(변경)신고**. The labels mean payment/notice/refund, national tax refund, then refund-account opening/change reporting. Submit an account change only after confirming the details, and ask whether a payment already sent or being processed needs separate action. A changed account setting is not proof that a previous transfer has been redirected.

### Fictional example: a smaller bank credit

A return result and later refund notice show KRW 300,000. The bank receives KRW 240,000. The taxpayer finds an official offset notice for KRW 60,000 and reconciles the difference. Without that notice, the next step would be to ask the responsible office for an explanation, not to label the difference a fee or file another return.

The figures illustrate document matching only. They do not predict the amount or treatment of a reader's refund.

### Prepare an enquiry the office can answer

Use a factual note: “I submitted [return/claim] for [income year] on [date], reference [number]. The refund record shows [tax type, amount and status]. My bank shows [date/amount or no matching credit]. Please confirm the unresolved step and whether you need additional evidence.”

Keep any notice reference and the office named on it. Do not send approval codes or a full bank statement through an unsolicited contact. If the portal cannot locate the record, keep the exact search period and tax type you used so the office can distinguish a search problem from a processing problem.

### Decide whether the task is complete

The trace is complete when the submitted return, official result and actual payment can be reconciled, or when the authority has identified a specific action still required. Keep that outcome with the original receipts. For a payment due rather than a refund, use the [tax-payment procedure](/procedures/how-to-pay-korean-tax-online/).
