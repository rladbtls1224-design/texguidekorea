### Describe the arrangement precisely

Write down who selected the residence, who signed the lease, who owns the deposit, who pays the landlord, whether the employee can keep any cash, and what happens when employment ends. These facts are more useful than a contract line that simply says housing provided.

### Split housing from related benefits

Separate rent, deposit financing, maintenance fees, utilities, internet, furniture, hotel stays, moving costs, broker fees, home-leave travel, and cash allowances. A single reimbursement report can contain items with different wage-income treatment.

### Match payroll to employer evidence

Ask payroll which amounts were included in gross wage income and which were excluded, and under what rule. Compare the answer with the employer's lease, payment ledger, and year-end withholding receipt. Correct inconsistencies before the annual settlement closes.

### Compare ordinary and flat-tax treatment

Eligible foreign workers considering the special flat rate should model the entire compensation package. A method that looks better using base salary alone may change when housing, allowances, equity compensation, and otherwise excluded items are added consistently.

### Keep documents through the assignment

Retain the original lease, renewals, deposit return, employer policy, reimbursements, and payroll decisions. Assignment extensions or a change from employer lease to personal lease can alter the analysis during the same tax year.
