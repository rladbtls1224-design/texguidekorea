### Identify the payment before choosing a form

Record who earns the income, who receives the cash, who pays it, where the underlying activity or asset is located, and whether an intermediary is involved. Then classify the payment: salary, independent services, interest, dividend, royalty, real-estate gain, securities gain, or another item. Similar commercial labels can fall under different treaty articles.

### Prove treaty residence and entitlement

Obtain a current certificate of residence from the competent tax authority of the claimed treaty country. Reconcile the legal name, address, tax number, and covered period with the contract and payment. For investment income, document the beneficial owner and any overseas investment vehicle rather than assuming the direct bank-account holder is entitled to relief.

### Coordinate with the Korean payer before payment

The payer or withholding agent needs enough time to review the application and attachments. Confirm the filing route, language, signatures, original-document requirements, and whether the payer will apply relief at source or withhold under domestic rules. Keep a complete copy and evidence of delivery.

### Separate relief at source from a refund claim

If Korean tax has already been withheld, do not overwrite the history with a new prospective form. Reconcile the gross income and tax actually withheld, obtain the withholding receipt, and use the applicable refund-request procedure. Treat deadlines as case-specific and verify them before relying on a treaty claim.
