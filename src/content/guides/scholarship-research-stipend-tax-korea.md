### Split the award into components

List tuition paid directly to the school, mandatory fees, books, travel, insurance, living allowance, expense reimbursement, and compensation for duties. One award can contain several tax categories. Ask the university for the Korean payment classification and any withholding statement.

### Look for a service requirement

Hours, deliverables, supervision, teaching, laboratory work, intellectual-property terms, or continued payment conditioned on work can point toward compensation. Reimbursement requires a business or academic purpose and supporting receipts. An unrestricted living payment needs its own classification rather than being treated as wages by default.

### Check the exact treaty

Some treaties have student, trainee, teacher, professor, or researcher articles. They may protect payments from abroad, named grants, limited local earnings, or qualifying teaching and research for a stated period. Nationality alone is insufficient; confirm prior residence, institution, purpose, amount limits, and duration.

### Match tax and immigration records

Confirm that the university's payroll report, Hometax data, visa work permission, bank receipts, and award documents tell the same story. If a payment was misclassified or withholding was omitted, resolve it before relying on a year-end certificate or May return.
