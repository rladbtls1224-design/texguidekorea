### Start with the business result

A low bank balance is a reason to investigate the accounts, not a completed tax calculation. List the business revenue for the year and the costs you believe relate to earning it. Keep contracts, invoices and payment evidence together so that each entry can be checked.

Separate owner withdrawals, loan movements and asset purchases from ordinary operating costs. Ask how an uncertain item should be treated instead of putting every outgoing payment into one expense category. For cost preparation, see [deductible business expenses](/guides/deductible-business-expenses-foreign-freelancers-korea/).

### Separate this year's loss from a carryforward

| Item | What the working paper should show |
| --- | --- |
| Current-year business result | Complete revenue and allowable cost calculation |
| Loss originating in a prior year | Original return and supporting computation |
| Amount already used | The year and return where it was deducted |
| Remaining balance | Opening balance less permitted amounts used |

Income Tax Act Article 45 sets income-category offset rules and generally provides a 15-year carryforward window. Confirm the originating year's rules and transitional provisions before setting an expiry date. Rental categories and financial-income restrictions require separate review; do not treat every loss as interchangeable.

### Check the filing method

Article 45 generally disallows carryforward deductions under estimated reporting, with a specified exception for unavoidable loss of records. Choosing a filing method can therefore affect more than the amount of expense evidence you need.

Before preparing the return, identify whether you will use qualifying books or an estimated method. Bring the prior return and loss schedule to your representative so the method and deduction can be reviewed together. See [bookkeeping requirements](/guides/bookkeeping-requirements-korea-foreign-freelancers/) for the records to organize.

### Build a loss-by-year schedule

Use a separate line for each originating year. Record the original loss, category, amounts used in each later return and balance remaining. Add references to the return and calculation files rather than keeping an unexplained total.

For example, a tracking sheet with an opening balance of KRW 4 million and a verified permitted use of KRW 1 million would show KRW 3 million remaining. This is bookkeeping arithmetic, not a conclusion that your particular loss qualifies or can be used in that year.

Reconcile the sheet whenever a return is amended or an assessment changes. Keep an explanation of the change so a later adviser can reconstruct the balance without guessing.

### Preserve the evidence

NTS record guidance describes five-year ordinary retention and 15-year retention where loss carryforward deductions are claimed. Keep digital copies with a consistent folder structure and confirm any additional requirements for your circumstances.

A useful folder contains:

1. The original loss-year books and supporting evidence.
2. The submitted return and receipt of submission.
3. The loss calculation and any professional adjustments.
4. Later returns showing deductions actually used.
5. Correspondence or assessments changing the amount.

A bank statement can support a payment trail, but keep the document explaining what the transaction was. Retain the calculation file as well as the final PDF.

### Connect the schedule to annual filing

Review the schedule during [global income tax filing](/guides/global-income-tax-filing-korea-foreigners/). Check the correct year's opening balance and investigate differences between your records and the return before submitting.

Treat refund expectations separately from the existence of a loss. Keep withholding and other prepaid tax records available for the final reconciliation. Business losses, share-sale losses and VAT calculations address different questions.

This guide was reviewed on October 15, 2026. Seek individual advice for mixed income, rental activities, old unreported losses, changed filing methods or missing records before claiming a deduction.
