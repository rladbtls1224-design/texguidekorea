### Classify the service before looking at payment

Describe exactly what was supplied: software development, design, advertising, consulting, licensing, platform content, education, performance, or another service. Different rules can apply, and a contract that combines services and intellectual property may need to be separated.

### Map where the service was supplied and used

Record where the freelancer performed the work, where the customer is established, where deliverables were received, and where the benefit was used. Remote digital delivery can still require a more specific legal analysis than simply drawing an arrow from Korea to another country.

### Match the statutory zero-rate route

The NTS lists exported goods, services rendered outside Korea, international transportation, and other qualifying foreign-exchange earning supplies as major zero-rate categories. Identify the exact route rather than citing the general idea of an export.

### Build the evidence pack with the invoice

Keep the contract, customer registration evidence, invoice, emails or system logs showing delivery and acceptance, bank remittance, currency conversion, and any required VAT attachment. Platform payouts should be reconciled from customer sale to fees to the final bank deposit.

### File zero-rated sales and input VAT together

Enter the zero-rated sales in the correct VAT return lines and attach the required evidence. Reconcile any input-VAT refund to valid Korean tax invoices or other accepted documents. If the evidence is incomplete, resolve it before treating the sale as zero-rated.
