### Confirm the activity is permitted

Before calculating tax, identify the property type, host status, lodging category, lease restrictions, building rules, and local reporting authority. Short-stay accommodation is not automatically permitted because a platform accepts the listing. Foreign residents should also confirm that their immigration status allows the business activity.

### Identify the actual supply

Map who contracts with the guest, who sets the rate, who provides cleaning and support, and who bears cancellations. This helps distinguish the host's accommodation revenue from platform, management, or agency services. It also prevents a long-term residential-rent analysis from being copied onto a short-stay lodging business.

### Reconcile gross bookings to cash

Use this monthly bridge:

`gross guest charges - refunds - platform fees - amounts withheld or collected - other adjustments = payout`

Keep the platform statement and bank receipt together. If the payout is in foreign currency, record the conversion date and rate consistently. Do not treat the final deposit as the only revenue record.

### Review every tax layer

The NTS has a dedicated tax guide for shared-accommodation operators, including the lodging-sharing business classification and registration process. Depending on the setup, the host may need business registration, income-tax filing, VAT review, cash-receipt or invoice procedures, and local taxes or fees. Residence status affects the filing method but does not automatically remove Korean tax from a Korea-based property or business.

### Allocate mixed-use costs

For a property also used personally, support the guest-use portion with an occupancy calendar and a reasonable method. Repairs, furniture, utilities, cleaning, management, interest, and depreciation can follow different rules. Keep invoices in the taxpayer's or business's correct name and do not deduct deposits or loan principal as ordinary expenses.
