### Label the money before labeling the tax

Write down why the sender paid the money and what right or obligation changed. Existing savings remain your asset; salary pays for work; business receipts pay an invoice; a gift transfers value without repayment; a loan creates an enforceable repayment obligation. One bank transfer can contain more than one category.

### Build a source-of-funds trail

Match the outgoing account, transfer confirmation, incoming account, and underlying documents. For old savings, retain statements showing the balance before Korean residence or before the transfer. For income, keep the contract and payment calculation. For a loan, document interest, maturity, security, and actual repayments rather than relying on a label.

### Apply residence and timing rules

Korean residents can face tax on worldwide income, while non-residents generally face Korean tax on Korean-source income. A foreign resident whose Korean domicile or residence period meets the limited-residence conditions may have a narrower rule for some foreign-source income, where payment or remittance into Korea matters. Trace amounts rather than treating the bank balance as one undifferentiated pool.

### Separate tax review from bank review

A Korean bank may ask for identification, transaction purpose, or source-of-funds documents under financial and foreign-exchange procedures. That review does not decide whether income or gift tax applies. Keep the same factual explanation and evidence for the bank, accountant, and tax filing.
