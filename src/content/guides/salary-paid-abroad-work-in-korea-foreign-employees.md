### The payment country is not the work country

For employment income, begin with the place where the employee actually performed the duties. If the employee worked from Korea, the related salary can be Korean-source even when the contract, payroll company, bank account, and currency are overseas. Build a day-by-day work calendar and allocate compensation consistently.

### Test domestic law and the treaty separately

First determine Korean residence and the domestic-law treatment. Then read the employment article in the treaty with the employee's country of residence. A short-term exemption commonly depends on presence, the employer, and whether a Korean entity or permanent establishment bears the compensation. Fewer than 183 days alone is not a complete answer.

### Reconcile the whole compensation package

Include regular salary, overseas bonuses, commissions, stock compensation, housing, education, tax reimbursements, and other benefits attributable to Korean duties. A split payroll should reconcile to one compensation ledger so the same item is neither omitted nor counted twice. Translate foreign-currency amounts using a supportable and consistent method.

### Decide how Korean tax is settled

A Korean employer or qualifying user company may have withholding duties. Other overseas-paid employment income may require settlement through an applicable taxpayer-association or return procedure. The correct route depends on the legal payer, economic cost, assignment arrangement, and current rules; ask the Korean payroll team for a written explanation of what is included.

### Coordinate foreign tax relief

If another country also taxes the same salary, check treaty relief and Korea's foreign-tax-credit rules. Keep final assessments, payment dates, refunds, and allocation workpapers. A credit generally cannot be supported merely by showing gross withholding that was later refunded.

### Practical review sequence

1. Mark every Korea workday and travel day.
2. List every compensation item, payer, currency, and payment date.
3. Identify which entity employs, directs, and economically bears the pay.
4. Test Korean residence and source rules.
5. Test every condition of the relevant treaty article.
6. Reconcile Korean withholding, foreign withholding, and any return still required.
