### Build one grant-to-sale timeline

For every award, list the grant date, vesting conditions, vesting date, exercise date if relevant, share delivery date, value used by payroll, sale date, and cash received. Attach the plan documents and brokerage confirmation to the same line item. This prevents option, RSU, and sale events from being mixed together.

### Identify the employment service period

An employee who moved countries during vesting should prepare a work-location calendar rather than allocating the award only by residence on the settlement date. Record Korea workdays, overseas workdays, assignment terms, and any leave or business travel that may affect the calculation.

### Reconcile payroll and personal reporting

Ask the Korean employer whether the equity amount was included in payroll, the year-end settlement, or a withholding receipt. Also ask the foreign parent what value and date it used. Differences do not automatically mean one record is wrong, but they must be explained before a return is filed.

### Preserve value and exchange-rate evidence

Keep the quoted share price, currency, exchange rate, and time zone used for the taxable event. When shares are later sold, the same records help establish basis and prevent the employment-income amount from being taxed again as part of the gain.

### Review treaty and credit relief

If another country taxed the same award, do not simply deduct the foreign tax from Korean tax. Confirm source allocation, treaty position, credit limits, currency conversion, and proof of final foreign tax paid.
