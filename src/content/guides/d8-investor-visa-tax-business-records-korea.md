### Identify the D-8 subtype and role

Record whether the applicant is an investor, owner-manager, dispatched executive, essential professional, individual-business investor, or venture founder. The evidence and the boundary between personal and company activity can differ by subtype.

### Trace investment funds end to end

Link the foreign remittance or in-kind investment to the Korean account, capital registration, and each material business expenditure. Keep remittance confirmations, foreign-exchange certificates, invoices, contracts, and bank withdrawals in one schedule.

### Maintain tax records as operating records

Reconcile sales to VAT returns, tax invoices, accounts receivable, and the income statement. Reconcile wages to payroll withholding. Clear national and local tax balances promptly so certificates can be issued when an extension or banking process begins.

### Separate company and investor money

Document salary, dividends, shareholder loans, reimbursements, rent, and capital contributions using the proper agreements and accounting entries. Personal use of the business account creates both tax and immigration-evidence problems.

### Build the extension file early

Before the extension window, obtain current certificates and compare them with the immigration checklist for the exact office and subtype. Official guidance allows additional documents to be requested, so leave time to correct discrepancies rather than submitting inconsistent figures.
