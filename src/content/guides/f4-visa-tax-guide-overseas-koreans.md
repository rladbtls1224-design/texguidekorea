### Decide tax residence from the facts

Review domicile, family, housing, occupation, expected stay, and days in Korea. Do not substitute the F-4 validity period for the tax-residence analysis. If two countries treat you as resident, check the relevant treaty tie-breaker separately.

### Sort Korean income by payment type

Salary, 3.3% contractor payments, business income, rent, interest, dividends, and capital gains follow different withholding and filing paths. Confirm what the payer reported to Hometax. A payment description on a bank transfer is not enough to determine its tax category.

### Review overseas income and accounts

For each overseas item, record the country, payer, income type, service location, payment date, and foreign tax. Determine whether Korean worldwide-income rules apply and whether the limited foreign-resident rule changes the scope. Large overseas accounts or structures can have separate reporting tests.

### Reconcile year-end settlement and May filing

Obtain every withholding receipt after a job change, compare it with Hometax, and identify income not included in employer settlement. Preserve residence, exchange-rate, foreign-tax, and expense records before the May filing window.
