### Build an item-by-item benefit inventory

List every cash and non-cash item paid by the Korean company, foreign employer, or related company. Include housing, utilities, school fees, relocation, home leave, tax preparation, tax equalization, club dues, per diems, cars, bonuses, and equity. Record the payer and economic cost bearer separately.

### Separate business expenses from personal benefits

A business trip undertaken for the employer is different from an allowance that increases the employee's personal spending power. Strong support normally includes a business purpose, company policy, dates, participants, receipts, approval, and a reasonable amount. A flat monthly payment with no accounting is harder to treat like an actual-expense reimbursement.

### Review common expat items carefully

| Item | Main question |
| --- | --- |
| Housing and utilities | Is the employer-provided arrangement covered by a specific rule, or is it employee compensation? |
| School fees | Is this a personal family benefit included in wages? |
| Relocation | Which costs serve the employer and which are personal moving costs? |
| Home leave | Does the trip satisfy the conditions for assignment-related actual-expense treatment? |
| Tax reimbursement | Is employer-paid employee tax grossed up as additional compensation? |
| Per diem | Does it support genuine business travel under a documented policy? |

### Reconcile all payroll locations

An overseas payment can still relate to Korean work. Combine home- and host-country payroll into one annual compensation schedule, then compare it with Korean withholding and year-end settlement. Track benefits paid directly to vendors as well as amounts reimbursed to the employee.

### Compare tax methods using total compensation

A foreign employee considering the special flat-tax method should model the entire package. That method generally changes access to exclusions, deductions, reductions, and credits. Compare the result for the current year instead of assuming the lower-looking headline rate is always better.
