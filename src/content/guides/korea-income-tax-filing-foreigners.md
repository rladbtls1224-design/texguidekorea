### Decide the filing route before opening a return

This page helps you answer **which filing obligation to check**. Once your route is clear, use the [global income tax preparation and submission checklist](/guides/global-income-tax-filing-korea-foreigners/) to reconcile figures and complete the filing record. For screen navigation, use the separate [Hometax procedure](/procedures/how-to-file-income-tax-on-hometax/).

Start with one row for every payer and income category in the relevant year. A useful first record is: payer, work or transaction, gross amount, withholding, document received, and whether that item was included in a settlement or return. Do not begin by adding only the amounts deposited into your bank account.

### Check salary settlement first

The [NTS overview](https://g.nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=7664&mi=2224) generally excludes salary-only earners with completed year-end settlement from a separate final return. It also lists exceptions, including multiple-payer income not properly combined and cases with no year-end settlement.

Ask payroll for the actual settlement result and withholding receipt. "Tax was deducted every month" does not answer whether annual settlement was completed or covered every employer.

| Your situation | First evidence to inspect | Next review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One salary payer, no other income, completed settlement | Final wage withholding receipt and settlement result | Confirm no filing exception or omitted item applies |
| Changed jobs during the year | Receipts from both employers and the final employer's combined settlement | Check whether the earlier salary was included |
| Two concurrent salary jobs | Each receipt and any combined settlement result | Review any salary that remains uncombined |
| Salary plus recurring independent work | Salary settlement plus client statements and work facts | Review the separate income classification and annual filing |
| Independent work without salary | Payer statements, contracts, withholding, and expense records | Check business/other-income treatment and the applicable return |
| Overseas income, arrival, departure, or uncertain residence | Residence facts, work locations, dates, and foreign records | Resolve income scope and treaty questions before choosing amounts |

The table is a route finder. It does not declare that every side payment is taxable in the same way or that every employee needs another return.

### Use concrete examples to test your records

**A job change with combined settlement:** Mira worked for Employer A and then Employer B. B's final receipt includes A's salary in the settlement. The next check is whether she has any other income or filing exception; the job change alone is not a reason to report the same salary twice.

**A job change with separate settlements:** Daniel has two employer receipts, and neither settlement includes the other employer's salary. He should investigate the final-return requirement using both receipts, rather than assuming two separate payroll settlements covered the combined annual position.

**Salary and independent work:** An employer settled Lina's wages, while a client separately reported her recurring translation work. Her salary paperwork does not explain the client's payment classification or business records. She needs an income-category review before preparing any comprehensive return.

These fictional examples illustrate questions to resolve. They do not calculate tax or establish an exemption for a reader's case.

### Resolve residence and income scope separately

The NTS's [current foreign-worker guidance](https://j.nts.go.kr/nts/na/ntt/selectNttInfo.do?mi=2201&nttSn=1347723) describes residence through a Korean domicile **or** at least 183 days of residence, with facts such as work, family, and assets relevant to the domicile analysis. Counting days alone can therefore miss the issue.

Prepare a short timeline if you arrived, left, worked remotely for a foreign employer, or have income from another country. Record where work was performed, when income arose, and what foreign tax documents exist. Use the [residence guide](/guides/korea-tax-residency-foreigners/) and [overseas income guide](/guides/overseas-income-tax-korea-foreigners/) to identify the questions to take to the NTS or a qualified adviser. A foreign bank account or foreign payer is not enough information to decide the Korean treatment.

### Match the income year to the correct filing season

The NTS [English filing information](https://www.nts.go.kr/english/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=238886&mi=40008) gives the ordinary comprehensive-income filing period as May 1–31 of the following year, with a separate June period for taxpayers subject to professional return review. Check the current notice for holidays, special deadlines, and your own category.

As of this review on **October 26, 2026**, the ordinary filing season for **2025 income** has passed. If a 2025 return was missed, investigate the [late-filing route](/guides/missed-may-tax-filing-deadline-korea/). For 2026 income, keep records during 2026 and check the applicable instructions for the following filing season. Do not select the current income year merely because that is the year in which you are using Hometax.

### Finish triage with a written decision

Before preparing figures, record the income year, each income category, whether salary was combined and settled, any unresolved residence issue, and the filing route you need to confirm. Then move to the [preparation checklist](/guides/global-income-tax-filing-korea-foreigners/).

If the only immediate task is to provide an existing income certificate to another institution, begin with [choosing the correct certificate](/guides/tax-documents-visa-extension-korea/). A document deadline should prompt an early check of the records; it should not determine which tax return you submit.
