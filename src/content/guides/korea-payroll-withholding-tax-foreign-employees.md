### Read the payslip in separate layers

Start with gross compensation, then identify taxable wage and salary income, non-taxable items, national income-tax withholding, local income tax, pension, health insurance, employment insurance, and other deductions. Only the tax lines belong in the income-tax reconciliation. Social-insurance contributions follow separate systems and should not be described as extra income tax.

### Confirm the simplified-table inputs

Korean payroll generally uses the simplified withholding table for ordinary monthly salary. Check the taxable monthly salary, number of family members eligible for the basic deduction, and eligible dependent children. A marriage, birth, departure, change in a dependent's income, or incomplete payroll form can make the table input stale.

### Choose 80%, 100%, or 120% for cash-flow reasons

The NTS foreigner year-end guide explains that an employee may choose 80% or 120% of the simplified-table amount instead of the standard amount. Paying less monthly can increase the balance due later; paying more can increase a refund or reduce the balance due. The election does not by itself change the annual deductions, credits, or tax brackets.

### Treat flat-tax withholding as a separate method

Foreign-worker flat-tax withholding is not the same as choosing 120% of the ordinary table. It has its own eligibility and form requirements and applies 19% to the covered wage and salary income under the special method. Because the flat method generally disallows deductions, exemptions, reductions, and credits, compare annual outcomes rather than only the monthly payslip.

### Reconcile at year-end and after job changes

At year-end settlement, compare final annual national and local income tax with the amounts withheld each month. If you changed jobs, give the new employer the prior-employer withholding receipt so both salary records can be combined where required. Keep the final annual receipt because it is the key record for refunds, later May filing, visa income proof, and corrections.
