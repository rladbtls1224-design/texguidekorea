### Identify the missed obligation

List each return, statement, invoice, and payment that may be late. Separate global income tax, VAT, withholding, capital gains, foreign financial account reporting, and local income tax. A correction that fixes only one obligation can leave another deadline open.

### Rebuild the correct base amount

Collect all income, expenses, withholding, credits, and prior payments for the affected period. Penalty calculations often start from unpaid tax, underreported tax, or omitted revenue, so a reliable underlying return is essential.

### Separate filing, reporting, and payment additions

Create individual lines for failure to file, underreporting, late payment, and any evidence or invoice issue. Record the statutory rate, base, day count, reduction, and source for each line. Do not calculate one combined percentage from memory.

### Check voluntary reduction timing

The timing of a late return or amended return can affect available reductions. Document when you discovered the issue, when you filed, and when you paid. Deliberate concealment or fraudulent conduct follows more serious rules and needs immediate professional advice.

### Close the national and local tax loop

After correcting national income tax, confirm the connected local income tax return and payment. Save acceptance receipts, payment confirmations, and the final account balance so a later visa, banking, or tax-payment certificate request is not surprised by an unresolved amount.
