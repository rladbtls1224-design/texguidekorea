### Fix the residence scope first

Prepare a year-by-year Korean residence timeline. Note domicile, occupation, family, housing, days, nationality, and the total Korean residence period relevant to the limited foreign-resident rule. If another country also claims residence, apply the treaty tie-breaker before assuming which return has priority.

### Build a rental schedule

Record gross rent, refundable and non-refundable deposits, vacancies, management fees, repairs, insurance, local taxes, mortgage interest, and depreciation. Keep personal-use periods separate. Reconcile the schedule to foreign bank and property-manager statements.

### Calculate a sale independently

Preserve original cost, acquisition expenses, capital improvements, depreciation history, sale price, and selling expenses. The foreign country's taxable gain may not equal the Korean gain because basis, exemptions, and exchange rates can differ.

### Claim relief with the right evidence

Match foreign tax to the same income and year reported in Korea. Keep the final foreign assessment and proof of payment, not only withholding or an estimate. If the foreign return changes later, review whether the Korean credit or return also needs correction.
