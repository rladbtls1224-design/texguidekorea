### Start with why and how you sell

Selling a used desk, bicycle, and kitchen items after personal use does not look like buying ten similar products to resell each month. Create a short fact record: why each item was acquired, how long it was used, why it was sold, and whether similar inventory was purchased for resale.

### Signs of a commercial activity

No single factor decides every case, but business risk grows when you:

- purchase or import inventory for resale;
- list similar new or refurbished items repeatedly;
- advertise continuously or maintain a storefront identity;
- price and operate for recurring profit;
- use dedicated storage, shipping, or staff; or
- combine platform activity with an existing retail business.

### Do not confuse deposits with profit

For a business, platform deposits should be reconciled to gross sales, refunds, fees, shipping, and supported cost of goods. For personal disposals, preserve original purchase information and the facts showing personal use. A bank total by itself cannot reliably prove either taxable profit or a non-business sale.

### Review connected duties

If the activity is a business, consider business registration, comprehensive income tax, VAT status, receipts, inventory records, and customs treatment. Foreign sellers must also confirm that their status of stay permits the activity. Tax registration does not expand immigration permission.

### Keep a simple evidence file

Export platform histories periodically. Save photos, descriptions, purchase receipts, settlement reports, and shipping records. For higher-value goods, keep serial numbers, authentication, repair records, and proof of personal use. Consistent evidence is especially helpful when many deposits look commercial without context.
